Title: Who Authored the Evidence? Actor–Evidence Coupling in AI-Assisted Software Acceptance

Authors: Ben Huffer

Primary category candidate: cs.SE (Software Engineering)

Abstract:
AI coding agents can change software, choose tests, summarize results, and write the review used to accept their own work. These steps can occur in one coupled process. Existing controls often address self-modification, where an actor edits the gate meant to constrain it. They do not always address evidence self-authorship. In that failure mode, the gate stays protected, but the actor writes the evidence it consumes. A confident hallucination can clear every gate for which it also wrote the input.

This paper treats acceptance of agent-assisted changes as a problem distinct from task performance. It defines an agent-specific form of evidence self-authorship and a five-axis profile for actor–evidence coupling. The axes are actor, context, mechanism, authority, and resource. The profile is not a score. Instead, an external, versioned policy can set evidence requirements based on consequence. The proposal adapts established review, configuration-management, verification, and assurance practice. A Git-native reference implementation shows that custody labels, coupling profiles, and decision state can be recorded, exercised, and checked for structural consistency. Those checks do not prove identity, truth, or independence. A twelve-scenario formative inspection supports design changes, not efficacy claims. The paper also states a falsifiable prediction and proposes a preregistered comparison of actor-controlled evidence with a defined separated-evidence condition.
