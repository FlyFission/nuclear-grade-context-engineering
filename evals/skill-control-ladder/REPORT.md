# Control Ladder: skill versus prompting

Generated by `scripts/analyze_ladder.py`. Do not hand-edit — rerun it.

## What this measures

The `skill-benchmark-pilot` in this repo compares a loaded skill against a bare
prompt with nothing appended, which measures skill-versus-nothing. This ladder
adds the intermediate arms needed to measure skill-versus-*prompting*. Every arm
uses an identical model, tool allowlist, budget cap, isolation flags, scenario,
and grading criterion; the only thing that varies is the text appended to the
system prompt.

| Arm | Appended to system prompt |
|---|---|
| `C0_bare` | bare prompt (nothing appended) |
| `C1_generic_nudge` | generic skill-agnostic reviewer nudge |
| `C2_description_only` | skill name + frontmatter description only |
| `C3_compressed` | skill compressed to <=5 imperative bullets |
| `C4_full_skill` | full SKILL.md body |

The C1 control text, identical for all skills and containing no vocabulary
from any of them:

> Before you answer, think like a careful senior reviewer accountable for the outcome. Identify what is missing, unstated, or assumed; what could go wrong and who would be affected; who needs to approve, be informed, or own the risk; and what evidence would actually back any claim you make. State the gaps, risks, and non-obvious consequences explicitly rather than leaving them implicit, and say plainly when something should not proceed as-is. Be specific and concrete rather than general.

## Primary result

Pre-registered hypothesis: across the 27-skill pool, the full
skill (C4) outscores the generic prompting control (C1) on paired per-skill
scores. Tested once.

- Mean paired difference: **+0.222** (score scale 0–1; YES=1, PARTIAL=0.5, NO=0)
- Skills where C4 > C1: **12** · C4 < C1: **0** · tied: **15**
- Two-sided exact sign test: **p = 0.0005**
- Two-sided paired permutation test (100,000 resamples, seed 20260726): **p = 0.0006**

C4 is **never worse** than C1 on any of the 27 skills. The 15 ties break down as follows, and the distinction matters:

**13 ties at the ceiling** — both arms scored 1.00, so the scenario is too easy to separate them. These are *uninformative*, not negative: the scenario cannot show a difference that may well exist. They are the top priority for harder replacement scenarios.

> `briefing-an-agent`, `checking-legal-and-safety-wording`, `checking-release-readiness`, `checking-what-a-change-affects`, `creating-change-records`, `deciding-who-decides`, `proving-claims`, `questioning-attitude`, `rating-change-risk`, `recording-what-an-agent-did`, `responding-to-incidents`, `stress-testing-agent-changes`, `vetting-outside-code-and-models`

**2 ties below the ceiling** — both arms scored the same and neither was perfect. Here the generic paragraph genuinely matched the skill with room to spare on both sides, which is the real finding:

- `organizing-project-folders` (C1 0.50 = C4 0.50)
- `using-nuclear-grade` (C1 0.67 = C4 0.67)

## Limitations

- **One subject model** (`claude-sonnet-5`), one scenario per skill, 3 trials per
  cell. Everything here is Sonnet-specific until replicated.
- **Criteria are not independent of the skills.** The pass criteria are reused
  unchanged from the pilot, and were authored by the same effort that wrote the
  skills. C1 partly defuses this — a generic paragraph that satisfies the
  criterion shows the criterion was measuring compliance rather than knowledge —
  but it does not remove it.
- **Trigger cases only.** Every scenario is one where the skill firing is the
  right behavior. Nothing here measures the cost of a skill firing when it should
  not, and nothing measures whether the right skill is selected out of the full
  library. Both remain open.
- **27 of the 29 skills on disk have a scenario.** `verifying-final-artifacts`
  has no eval coverage at all; `reviewing-code-quality` is covered by the separate
  3-task pilot rather than this pool.
- **The C1 text is one specific paragraph.** A different generic control would
  move these numbers. It was written to be strong on purpose; a weak control
  would inflate every skill's measured effect.
