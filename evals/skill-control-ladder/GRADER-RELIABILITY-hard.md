Generated by `scripts/grader_reliability.py`. Do not hand-edit.

Measured on the binary rubric, per individual check.

**How to read the disagreement rate.** It bounds how precisely a SINGLE cell
is measured — it does not set a floor under the pooled effect size. Grader
noise is roughly independent across cells, so averaging over trials, checks,
and skills shrinks its contribution to the pooled mean well below the
per-check rate; what it inflates is variance, which the paired permutation
test already reflects. An earlier draft of this file claimed any effect
smaller than the disagreement rate was unmeasurable. That was wrong, and
wrong in the direction of understating the results, so it is corrected here
rather than quietly dropped. The correct use of these numbers is as a check
that the grader is a stable instrument at all, and as a caution against
reading any individual cell as precise.

κ interpretation (Landis & Koch): <0.40 poor, 0.40–0.60 moderate,
0.60–0.80 substantial, >0.80 almost perfect.

A symmetric test-retest disagreement (roughly equal counts in both
directions) means grader noise adds variance without pushing scores
systematically up or down, so it does not bias any arm relative to another —
all arms are graded by the same instrument. A lopsided cross-model
disagreement means the two models differ in strictness; that shifts absolute
scores but, again, applies equally across arms, so between-arm differences
survive it better than absolute levels do.
