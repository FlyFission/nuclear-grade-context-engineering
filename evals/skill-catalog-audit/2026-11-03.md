# Skill catalog audit: use, value, overlap, consolidation

Date: 2026-11-03 · Scope: the 30 directories under `skills/` (the in-progress `skills/checking-facts/` is excluded on purpose) · Mode: read-only audit, no repo files changed.

> **Provenance and coupling.** Written by an AI subagent briefed by the same session that wrote
> `checking-facts`, `verification-methods-and-depth.md`, and the `--two-party` wiring in this branch.
> The orchestrator spot-checked these claims against the source files: the trigger overlap between
> `declaring-intent` and `double-checking-before-acting`; no `NUCLEAR-GRADE-CLOSED:` packet anywhere;
> the v1c rows for `choosing-what-to-control`, `closing-stale-packets`, and `tracking-deficiencies`;
> and the 3/3 co-use of the CM records. That spot-check is a **check** (rung 1 in
> `docs/02-operating-system/verification-methods-and-depth.md`), not an independent review. Four clean
> spot-checks also bound this report's error rate only below about 53% (one-sided 95%), so treat every
> row as a hypothesis until a merge's own evaluation confirms it. Every
> merge below is a **proposal**. Each one needs its own Standard change record and human review
> before any skill is removed, as `docs/05-reference/independent-skill-benchmark-protocol.md` requires.
>
> **Update after the audit:** `checking-facts` has since landed as skill 31, and `checking-source-claims`
> and `proving-claims` now point to it in their When Not to Use sections. The audit's "settle its
> boundary first" note is handled for those two. Its relation to a merged public-claims skill is
> still open.

**Question asked:** "Do all of the skills actually 1) get used and 2) add value? Can we make it clearer what skills do and/or consolidate some?"

**Short answer.**
1. **Use.** No skill is named as *used on other work* in any change packet. Every non-boilerplate mention of a skill name in the 55 repo packets and the 1 worked-example packet is a packet that writes, edits, maps, or evaluates that skill. Use shows up only through **template sections and record files**. The four core templates (`risk.md`, `verification.md`/`proof.md`, `ship.md`, plus the packet shell) appear in nearly every packet. The three CM records appear together in 3 packets, and `opex.md` in 2. **No packet has ever produced a** `turnover.md`, `self-check.md`, `questioning-attitude.md`, `supplier-trust.md`, `intent.md`, `deficiency.md`, `decision-authority.md`, `incident.md`, `red-team.md`, `execution-trace.md`, `wbs.md`, panel, or context-pack record. Packets explicitly declined four of these: 21, 19, 20 and 19 times.
2. **Value.** In the pooled confirmatory run, the full skill beats a strong generic reviewer paragraph by **+0.102** (v1c; 19 better / 1 worse / 7 tied, p<0.0001). It does **not** replicate on 7 calibrated hard scenarios (**−0.065**, 3 better / 4 worse, p=0.38). At pool level, a ≤5-bullet compression matches the full body (v1c C4−C3 = **−0.005**). Per-skill results are too thin (n=3, one scenario) to support strong per-skill verdicts. The verdicts below are therefore labelled with their evidence and stay provisional.
3. **Consolidation.** The evidence supports going from **30 to 22 skills** with 8 merges, grouped into 7 families. The Core 7 does not change. The strongest moves are merges where the triggers overlap, nothing in the packets uses the merged skill, and the eval data shows the extra body text adds nothing measurable.

**How to read the negative pool:** every scenario sits inside the skill's own "do not use" territory, and the skill is force-loaded. A **higher** score means a more proportionate answer, so **C4 < C1 is the cost finding** (the skill made the answer heavier than the generic prompt did). The pre-registered good outcome was a null. Per `README.md`, the pool also has a fixture confound: an empty work dir makes some answers ask clarifying questions. Treat it as directional only.

**Verdict rules used here.** These are stated so they can be challenged.
- **KEEP**: some usage signal, at least one positive comparison (v1c C4>C1, hard C4>C1, or a pilot Gate-1 win at n=5), and no C4<C1 interval that excludes zero.
- **KEEP-SHARPEN**: worth keeping, but a cheaper arm (C2 description-only or C3 compressed) matched or beat C4, or a C4<C1 signal exists. The fix is to trim or re-scope.
- **MERGE→X**: substantial trigger or purpose overlap with X, plus weak or no distinct usage. The content folds into X.
- **DEMOTE / RETIRE**: none assigned outright. Two conditional DEMOTE candidates are named.
- **NO-EVIDENCE**: cannot tell. No trigger-case eval, or only ceiling cells, plus no usage signal.

## 3. Verdict rationale (one paragraph per non-trivial call)

- **`questioning-attitude`, KEEP-SHARPEN.**
  - The generic control C1 is essentially a questioning-attitude paragraph: "identify what is missing, unstated, or assumed …". It scores .93, and bare scores 1.00.
  - The full skill adds +.07. C3 already reaches 1.00.
  - Forced-load proportionality drops it to .40, against .60 for the generic paragraph.
  - Its dedicated record was declined in all 21 packets that list it.
  - Keep it as a Core disposition, but compress the body to the decision question, the load-bearing assumptions, the decision-changing fact, and the next route (the static audit proposed the same).
- **`using-nuclear-grade`, KEEP-SHARPEN.**
  - It has the lowest pool scores among routed skills (C4 .33), and C3 (.50) beats C4.
  - It duplicates `rating-change-risk`'s mode classification: "administrative floor" appears 6× here and 2× there, and both carry the same Standard-plus tripwire list.
  - It routes to no skill by name.
  - Recommended change: make it a thin router. Hand mode selection to `rating-change-risk` and carry a trigger→skill table lifted from the `CORE.md` matrix.
- **`rating-change-risk`, `proving-claims`, `checking-release-readiness`, KEEP.**
  - These three carry the template-driven usage: 55, 55 and 39 packets.
  - `proving-claims` has the largest proportionality gain (+.47*) and a clean structural recheck (5/5 vs 0/5).
  - `rating-change-risk` and `checking-release-readiness` sit at the v1c ceiling but are mildly positive on hard (+.06 each) and won Gate 1 against bare.
- **`double-checking-before-acting`, KEEP-SHARPEN.** It has the strongest over-process signal in the catalog: negative pool .67→.27 (−.40, CI [−.67,−.07]). C3 (.93) beats C4 (.87). As the Core habit it stays, and it absorbs `declaring-intent` (see §4). Merging should shrink the text, not add to it.
- **`staying-on-mission`, NO-EVIDENCE (value).** Every v1c arm scores 1.00, the negative pool ties, and the pilot result is a thin margin. It does have the clearest non-overlapping trigger (drift, loops, gradual erosion of standards, no other skill claims these), and its anchor field is used in 20 packets. It needs a harder scenario, not a merge.
- **`learning-from-experience`, KEEP.** It is positive in every comparison (v1c +.13, pilot 3/3 vs 0/3, Haiku replicates), and it is the only incident-family skill with real record use (`opex.md` ×2). It becomes the "after-the-fact" target. Keep C3>C4 in mind and trim while merging.
- **`creating-change-records`, KEEP-SHARPEN.** It is used in every packet, but through `ng new` and the templates. There is no evidence the *skill text* adds anything:
  - v1c ceiling on all arms.
  - Hard pool C4 .56 < C1 .67, with description-only C2 at .94.
  - The pilot win needed a re-graded scope and did not replicate on Haiku.
  - Conditional DEMOTE candidate: if it still ties C2 after absorbing `closing-stale-packets`, move the content into `templates/README.md` and the `ng new` help.
- **`closing-stale-packets`, MERGE→`creating-change-records`.** The description alone (C2) scores 1.00, equal to C4. The 2,636-token body adds no measured value over the 114-token description. In the negative pool C4 (.67) sits below C1 (.73). The repo has never closed a packet with `NUCLEAR-GRADE-CLOSED:` (the only hit is a test-scenario row in `ci-scaffold-generator/verification.md`). It belongs as a short "closing a packet" section of the packet-lifecycle skill.
- **`choosing-what-to-control`, MERGE→`recording-a-known-good-version`.** The three CM records (`controlled-items.md`, `change-impact.md`, `baseline.md`) appear together in exactly 3 packets: `cm-public-readiness`, `glean-nuclear-leadership` and `doctrine-spine-influence`. They are never used apart. The controlled-items list is the scope of the baseline. Keep `choosing-what-to-control`'s process, because it has the measured lift (+.27*, C3 = C4). Do **not** compress the baseline half naively: `recording-a-known-good-version` C3 = 0.00 while C4 = .33.
- **`checking-what-a-change-affects`, KEEP (separate).** It makes a distinct per-item decision (update / leave / defer / block). It has the best CM pilot result (Gate 1: 5/5 vs 1/5+4p) and is positive in the negative pool (+.20). The hard pool is flat (.28→.22). The pilot's Gate-2 overlap sweep also found its pairing with release readiness to be a legitimate pipeline.
- **`breaking-down-the-work`, MERGE→`organizing-project-folders` (lower confidence).**
  - Each claims the other's trigger. `breaking-down` triggers on "a folder tree or repo structure is about to be designed"; `organizing` triggers on "turning a work breakdown into real folders".
  - `CORE.md` routes them together in the workflow-architecture row.
  - Together they are 6,810 body tokens, the 2nd and 3rd largest.
  - `wbs.md` has never been produced.
  - `breaking-down` C3 (.80) beats C4 (.73), and its pilot win is a thin margin.
  - Caveat: the delegable-slice idea now also lives in the `plan.md` build-sequence columns (packet `incorporate-planning-lessons`). The merged body must be compressed hard to fit `skill_body_max` 3,900.
- **`briefing-an-agent`, MERGE→`handing-off-work` (contested).**
  - Evidence against keeping it separate:
    - Hard pool C4 .22 vs C1 .56 (−.33, CI [−.56,−.17]), the only CI-excluding negative in that pool, measured on today's text.
    - In v1c, bare C0 (.89) beats C4 (.67).
    - It tied in both pilot rounds.
    - No context-pack record has ever been produced.
  - Evidence for keeping it separate: after the July amendment it won 5/5 vs 0/5 on its "true niche".
  - `handing-off-work` is the stronger skill in every comparison (v1c +.28*, Gate 1 5/5 vs 0/5). The generated cards `ng-turnover` and `ng-context-pack` already each reference both skills.
  - One skill with two entry states (before start / mid-flight) settles the overlap that the July amendment tried to fix by drawing a boundary.
- **`deciding-who-decides`, KEEP-SHARPEN.** It is the only v1c skill where C4 (.78) scores below C1 (.83), and compression beats the full text by .16 (C3 .94). At 3,212 tokens it is the 4th-largest body. Its decision (who holds authority) is distinct from mode (`rating-change-risk`, per the pilot's Gate-2 sweep) and from pre-action checking, so keep it, compressed.
- **`declaring-intent`, MERGE→`double-checking-before-acting`.**

## 7. Limitations (read before acting)

- **Single model family.** Subject `claude-sonnet-5`, grader Haiku 4.5 (cross-model κ .78–.80 with Sonnet as grader). There are no non-Anthropic models. Only 4 skills were checked on a second subject (Haiku), and 1 of the 4 did not replicate.
- **Repo-authored rubrics.** The v1/v1c criteria came from the effort that wrote the skills, and they reward the repo's own vocabulary. The hard and negative pools were written from descriptions only, which is less circular but still in-house. No blinded human grading exists. The ladder README says it measures "instruction adherence", not correctness.
- **n = 3, one scenario per skill.** Per-skill bootstrap intervals are unreliable, and a CI that excludes 0 at n=3 is a weak signal. 0 of 47 pilot tests survive multiple-comparison correction. 17 of 27 pilot round-1 scenarios had a bare baseline ≥ 50%, so ceiling effects dominate.
- **Direct injection, no selection.** Every eval hands the model the right skill. Routing precision and recall across the full catalog, which is the cost that consolidation most directly lowers, is **unmeasured**. The 164 routing cases have never been scored against observed routes. The negative pool is forced-load proportionality with a known empty-fixture confound, not real over-firing.
- **Usage proxies are dogfooding.** One small, mostly single-maintainer documentation repo, using its own templates. There is no adopter telemetry. Zero packets for an incident or deficiency skill can just mean "no incidents here". A template section in every packet counts as "used" even if the skill was never loaded.
- **Shallow git clone** (50 commits). Commit counts and first dates are truncated at 2026-06-16.
- **Pilot text drift.** The July pilot predates several skill amendments (2026-07-19 through 08-01). The ladder pin (2026-10-13) matches today's text for 29 of 30 skills.
- **Unaudited.** `skills/checking-facts/` (in progress elsewhere) and the concurrent edits to `nuclear_grade/ng_validate.py` and `two_party.py` in the working tree.

This audit does not create formal verification and validation, compliance, certification, safety, security, or regulatory adequacy evidence.
